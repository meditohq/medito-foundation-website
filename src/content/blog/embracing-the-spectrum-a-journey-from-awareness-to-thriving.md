Every year on April 2nd, the global community comes together to observe World Autism Awareness Day—a day dedicated to raising awareness about autism and advocating for the acceptance and support of people with autism. This year, we at Medito Foundation are proud to align with the theme of 2024: "Awareness – Acceptance – Appreciation: Moving from surviving to thriving." Our mission resonates deeply with this theme, as we believe in fostering an inclusive society where individuals with autism can not only live freely but also truly thrive.

### Understanding Autism

Autism, also known as autism spectrum disorder (ASD), is a developmental disability that affects how people communicate, interact with others, learn, and behave. It's characterized by difficulties with social communication, such as facial expressions, movements, gestures that often don't match what's being said, and restricted or repetitive behaviours. About [1 in 100 children has autism](https://www.crossrivertherapy.com/autism-statistics), and characteristics may be detected in early childhood. However, generally autism is not diagnosed until much later. The abilities and needs of people with autism vary and can evolve over time. With more than 70 million people worldwide living with autism, the need for supportive activities and inclusive practices has never been more critical.

### A Global Overview

The 2024 observance of World Autism Awareness Day is set to provide a global perspective on Autism directly from people with autism themselves. This initiative promises a panel of people with autism from around the world who will share insights into the state of autism awareness, acceptance, and appreciation in their respective regions. Such an approach not only empowers individuals with autism by giving them a platform to share their experiences but also enriches our understanding of autism across different cultural and geographical contexts.

### Meditation, Mindfulness, and Autism

[Research](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3420737/) and [anecdotal evidence](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23311908.2015.1071028) suggest that meditation and mindfulness practices can offer several benefits for individuals with autism. These practices can help manage stress, reduce anxiety, and improve focus and concentration. Mindfulness, in particular, can aid in developing a better understanding and awareness of one's body and emotions, which can be particularly [beneficial](https://www.myautism.org/news-features/meditation-and-mindfulness-for-autism) for those who experience sensory processing differences.

We believe that by integrating mindfulness and meditation into the lives of individuals with autism, we can support their journey towards thriving, not just surviving. Our goal is to create an environment that acknowledges and appreciates the unique perspectives and contributions of autistic individuals, promoting a sense of belonging and acceptance.
